Aestus Whitepaper

Measure the tide. Publish the state.
Aestus Research · research@aestus.fun
Draft v0.1 — July 2026

Abstract. Markets do not live separate lives. Bitcoin, equities, gold, crude, and the dollar breathe to one rhythm — the expansion and contraction of global liquidity. Traders feel the connection as weather. Aestus treats it as a measurement problem. We estimate the unobserved macro state from a public cross-asset panel — time-varying correlations, a hidden-Markov regime model, lead-lag analysis, and a composite liquidity reading — and intend to publish the result as an on-chain primitive any application can consume. An optimistic-oracle design is the research path for securing that feed. This paper specifies the model and the intended protocol. The Aestus token, if and when it exists as a fair-launch community flag (Section 9), is kept honestly separate from the unbuilt machinery.

Keywords: cross-asset correlation, risk-on/risk-off, regime switching, hidden Markov models, global liquidity, optimistic oracles, DeFi macro primitives.

1. Introduction

Open a feed and it looks like noise. One day Bitcoin leads; the next, gold; equities sell while crude tells its own story. Asset by asset, the books seem independent. They are not. Under the surface, the same hand moves them.

That hand is liquidity — the looseness or tightness of money, shaped by the dollar, real yields, and central-bank balance sheets. When the tap opens, risk rises together. When it closes, capital retreats into safety. The mutual balancing every macro observer senses is not mysticism. It is a switch, statistical and time-varying.

Something about treating that switch as story always bothered me. So I started asking a different question: can the unobserved macro state be measured from public data and published as a scalar anyone can consume — without pretending to forecast prices or control markets?

Aestus is the answer I kept arriving at. Named for the Latin tide and surge, it does for global risk what a tide gauge does for water: read how high the water is, and whether it is coming in or going out. The reading is the product.

1.1 Why on-chain

DeFi has excellent price oracles and almost no macro oracle. A lending market can price ETH to eight decimals and still have no notion of whether the whole field is risk-seeking or risk-averse — the latent variable that governs correlation, volatility, and tail risk across collateral at once. In a risk-off cascade, “uncorrelated” books become one book. A trustworthy on-chain macro reading is not a convenience. It is a missing safety primitive.

1.2 Contributions

  • A layered cross-asset macro model that outputs a discrete regime, a continuous liquidity index, and a sensitivity decomposition from public data.
  • A staked optimistic oracle design (research vision) for bringing that output on-chain with a dispute path.
  • An honestly scoped community flag — Section 9 states what the token is and is not, separate from unbuilt protocol ambitions.
  • A staged path from a single signed reporter toward multi-operator consensus.

2. The Macro Aestus

Model the global market as one body of water. A single tide — liquidity — raises and lowers the fleet. On top of the tide, currents create relative motion: gold against Bitcoin, a strong dollar dragging risk down, oil tracking growth and geopolitics. Three layers:

  • Gravity (liquidity). Dollar index, real yield, balance-sheet proxies — the slow common force.
  • Switch (regime). Risk-on / neutral / risk-off — the medium-frequency rotation of capital between risk and safety.
  • Character (residual). What remains after gravity and regime are removed — each asset’s temperament.
In one line: every asset is first pushed by the same liquidity tide, then rotated by a risk switch; only what remains is its own character. Aestus estimates the first two layers and reports them.

2.1 Notation

Let the panel contain N assets with log returns ri,t. Macro drivers (DXY, real yield, liquidity proxy) sit in mt. The unobserved regime is st ∈ {1,…,K}. Headline outputs: regime label st, risk-on probability Pon(t), and the Macro Aestus Index T(t) ∈ [0, 100].

The engineering problem is temporal alignment: crypto never sleeps; equities and macro observe sessions. We resample to a common daily grid, flag stale observations, and compute returns only over genuine trading transitions. Inputs are content-addressed so a given index value is reproducible from a fixed set.

3.2 Time-varying correlation

The “balancing” is the time variation of the correlation matrix Rt. Static correlations lie. We estimate conditional volatilities with univariate GARCH(1,1) and conditional correlation with DCC:

Qt = (1 − a − b) Q̄ + a (εt−1 εt−1⊤) + b Qt−1 (1)

Mean pairwise correlation ρ̄(t) is one of the strongest single stress indicators: it rises toward one when the market sells as a bloc.

3.3 Regime detection

Risk regime is the hidden state of a K-state HMM on joint returns and volatility. States emit characteristic signatures modelled as multivariate Gaussians; parameters via Baum–Welch; path via Viterbi. Default K = 3 — risk-on, neutral, risk-off — labeled post hoc by volatility and mean-correlation.

P(st = j | st−1 = i) = Aij ,  rt | st = s ~ 𝒩(μs, Σs) (2)

3  θ ← BaumWelch(X_window, K)
4  γ_t ← Forward-Backward P(s_t | X)
5  s_t ← argmax γ_t ;  P_on(t) ← γ_t[risk-on]
output: s_t , P_on(t)

3.4 Lead-lag

Directional flow via Granger causality and transfer entropy. Aggregated across the panel, this yields a directed graph — who leads whom — that correlation alone cannot give.

TY→X = Σ p(xt+1, xt, yt) · log [ p(xt+1|xt,yt) / p(xt+1|xt) ] (3)

3.5 The Macro Aestus Index

Headline scalar T(t) combines slow liquidity gravity with medium-frequency regime probability. Before normalization:

T̃(t) = α·L(t) − β·Yreal(t) − γ·DXY(t) + δ·Pon(t) (4)
T(t) = 100 · Φrank( T̃(t) ; window ) (5)

So T ∈ [0, 100] reads as a tide gauge. High: abundant liquidity and risk appetite. Low: draining tide and flight to safety. Weights are fixed by published methodology and changed only under versioned governance with a timelock — when that governance exists.

4. Empirical Results

Backtests on a multi-year daily panel show the expected signatures: mean pairwise correlation spikes in risk-off windows; the decoded HMM states align with known stress episodes; β-to-ΔT ranks risk assets above hedges. Figures in the Terminal are illustrative samples, not a claim of live production telemetry.

Live data at genesis. Until then, treat every chart on this site as a map of the method — not the territory of a running feed.

5. The Aestus Oracle

Price oracles already exist. Aestus needs a different object: a slowly moving macro state that must be hard to game and easy to dispute. The intended design is a staked optimistic oracle — operators post a signed report; a challenge window opens; bonded challengers can force a dispute game.

Let me be plain: Section 5 is a research vision. It describes what the network could become. It is not a representation that any on-chain contract is live, nor that any token already carries staking, slashing, or governance rights.

5.1 Report shape

{ regime, index T, P_on, input_hash, methodology_version, sig }

Consumers pin a methodology version. Changing the model is not a silent edit; it is a version bump with a timelock once governance exists.

5.2 Staging

  1. Single signed reporter + public reproducibility of inputs.
  2. Multi-operator median with deviation penalties.
  3. Toward verifiable computation where the economics justify it.

7. Use Cases

  • Risk engines — collateral haircuts that widen when T drains and ρ̄ spikes.
  • Vaults / perps — regime-aware leverage caps without inventing a price.
  • Analytics — Terminal and explorers as the public face of the reading.
  • Research — open methodology so the idea can be judged on merits.

8. Security

Threats worth naming: poisoned inputs, operator collusion, model risk, oracle latency. Mitigations in the vision: content-addressed inputs, stake-weighted median, open dispute game, versioned methodology, and the admission that macro state is a slow variable — not a high-frequency price.

No design removes model risk. Open methods and pinned versions are how consumers choose what to trust.

9. The Aestus Token

This section states plainly what the token is, why it would exist, and what it is not. We would rather be explicit and unexciting here than leave a gap for assumptions.

What it is — and is not

It is a community flag and a signal: a way for people who share the thesis to find each other, and a marker of being early to it.

It is not a security, a share of any entity, a claim on revenue, or a promise of price appreciation. It does not entitle holders to any future token, airdrop, or 1:1 migration. We make no such commitment.
